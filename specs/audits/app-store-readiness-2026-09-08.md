## 1) Executive Summary

- QuoteMax is a tradie account app for drafting/reviewing quotes, handling customer conversations and following work/payment progress. The principal review journeys are account access/onboarding, draft-to-review-to-send, and returning to customer/payment activity.
- **Do not submit the current candidate as deployment-ready.** Confirmed account, privacy and recovery defects remain; the installed iOS build and review environment are unverified.
- **Top risk 1: no usable QuoteMax account deletion journey.** Native signup is present, but Account offers a business summary, a dashboard handoff and biometric settings. No native delete-account entry or complete tenant deletion contract was found.
- **Top risk 2: legal/privacy presentation is incomplete.** No explicit privacy policy link was found on native welcome, signup, account, help or support surfaces. The web policy source still renders placeholder legal identity/contact fields. No explicit third-party AI sharing disclosure/permission was found before native AI workflows.
- **Top risk 3: the subscription offering is unresolved.** Store purchasing is deliberately disabled pending server reconciliation; existing Stripe customers can open a portal capable of subscription management. Select a compliant launch model and make every visible billing path match it.
- **Fast win 1:** implement Clerk-native recovery. The current Clerk sign-in screen opens a Supabase password reset page, so a Clerk-only account cannot recover its actual native credential there.
- **Fast win 2:** complete legal identity/contact content and put accessible legal links on signed-out and signed-in surfaces; inventory actual mobile/backend data recipients.
- **Fast win 3:** prepare a dedicated review account, realistic demo data and a signed production-like iOS build. Confirm the actual build uses Xcode 26 and the iOS 26 SDK, then exercise it on physical devices.
- Native signup, email verification, duplicate-account resume, biometric lock, cache isolation, push plumbing, support forms, provider-return handling and Sentry wiring already exist. They need targeted completion and device evidence, not wholesale rebuilding.
- Full public marketing/customer/admin parity is a product-scope choice. An owner-focused first release can leave customer installation-free flows and staff administration on the web if its mobile feature claims and links are accurate.

## 3) Detailed Findings

### Privacy & Data Handling

**A. Legal content and accessible policy.** The native route registry has welcome/support/auth and owner sections but no legal destination (`mobile/src/lib/destinations.ts:24`). Inspection of welcome, signup, help, support and account found no explicit privacy or terms link. Linking the current web policy alone would remain incomplete: `web/app/legal/_components/company.ts` supplies placeholder company name, ABN, address and privacy email. The policy enumerates Supabase, Twilio, voice/AI providers, Stripe, Resend and Vercel (`web/app/legal/privacy/page.tsx:105`), while this mobile app also uses Clerk, Expo push, optional RevenueCat and optional Sentry. Complete the published policy with the real provider/data inventory and accessible entrypoints. Verify signed-out and signed-in reachability and the actual production page.

**B. Third-party AI sharing.** Job quoter collects site/customer details, then triggers drafting; broader tools process plans/photos/documents. No consent/disclosure component was found in native JSX. This is a missing presentation boundary, not proof that every collected field is transmitted to every model. Trace each actual backend payload and identify the minimum data shared. Add a clear explanation and explicit consent before personal data leaves for AI processing, with a useful decline path where feasible. Test that declining prevents the relevant network operation, acceptance is tied to the disclosed purpose, and user/account changes do not inherit another person's consent. Apple's current data-sharing rule expressly includes third-party AI. [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**C. Persistence and privacy controls already present.** Clerk uses its token cache; legacy token storage uses SecureStore. Server state persists in AsyncStorage for up to 24 hours with a Clerk identity buster (`mobile/src/lib/query.ts:16,24`; `mobile/src/app/_layout.tsx:157`). Sign-out clears in-memory query data, persisted query data, saved estimator/commercial-paint run IDs, acquisition state and legacy credentials (`mobile/src/lib/account-storage.ts:19`). The public support draft uses SecureStore and clears after a confirmed send (`mobile/src/features/support/contact-draft.ts:42,54,63`). It is intentionally separate from the account cache, but shared-device retention and a visible discard option deserve verification. None of these code facts establishes encryption of the complete query cache or correct app-switcher snapshots.

**D. Monitoring.** Sentry is configured conditionally from a valid public DSN, with PII, screenshots, view hierarchy, performance tracing and breadcrumbs disabled, plus event sanitisation (`mobile/src/lib/monitoring.ts:43-59`). This is meaningful implementation. Whether release credentials, symbol upload and alerts work remains unverified. `SENTRY_DISABLE_AUTO_UPLOAD=true` in production is a diagnostic limitation unless another tested upload path exists; it is not itself an Apple policy violation.

**E. Privacy manifests and store privacy answers.** This is a generated-native-project setup; absence of a top-level `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` does not prove absence in the archive. Installed Expo FileSystem and AsyncStorage packages contain manifests. Inspect the complete native dependency archive and its aggregated privacy report, including Clerk/Sentry/RevenueCat and transitive dependencies. Reconcile app privacy answers with the actual data paths and third-party SDK behavior. Do not add tracking prompts simply because analytics exists; first establish whether tracking occurs. [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [Third-party SDK requirements](https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/)

### Permissions & Entitlements

Camera, photo-library and Face ID descriptions are purpose-specific in `mobile/app.json:80-87`. Image capture currently requests camera permission and selects images (`mobile/src/lib/media.ts:260,275`). Image-picker's microphone option is unspecified; SDK 54 adds recording permission by default. Disable it if no recording workflow is intended. [Expo SDK 54 ImagePicker](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v54.0.0/sdk/imagepicker/)

Biometric opt-in proves authentication before enabling. The gate supports cancellation/retry, device passcode fallback and a 30-second grace period; it deliberately treats biometrics as a privacy overlay, with Clerk remaining the real identity boundary (`mobile/src/lib/lock.ts:36,57`; `mobile/src/features/auth/BiometricGate.tsx:43`). Verify cold-start content flash, disabled enrollment, inactive/background transitions and app-switcher snapshots. Face ID requires a development/installed build for meaningful testing. [Expo SDK 54 LocalAuthentication](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v54.0.0/sdk/local-authentication/)

Push registration is implemented: contextual opt-in, refusal handling, Expo token registration, authenticated token retirement, foreground display and cold/warm tap routing (`mobile/src/lib/notifications.ts:54,118,172,199`). This does not prove APNs credentials, FCM setup, device delivery or scheduled receipt processing. `web/app/api/cron/push-receipts/route.ts` exists; `web/vercel.json` does not schedule it. An external scheduler may exist, so classify scheduling as unverified rather than certainly absent. Android FCM is a Play/Android release concern, not a blocker for an iOS-only submission.

### Monetization (IAP/Subscriptions)

There are two different payment domains. The QuoteMax Starter/Pro/Crew subscription unlocks software functionality. Customer deposits, inspections and final payments pay for physical trade work. Those trade payments should not be migrated to StoreKit. Apple's rules distinguish outside-app services from digital access and permit qualifying free companion apps with no purchase path or purchase call to action. US storefront rules also differ from general external-link restrictions. [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Current implementation:** `mobile/src/lib/purchases.ts` lazily loads RevenueCat, configures public SDK keys, synchronises Clerk identity and caches customer information. It contains paywall, customer-center and restore helper functions (`:196,209,225`). Searches found no user-facing callers of those helpers. Billing reads the server's Stripe-derived status and its purchase gate always returns `allowed:false` (`mobile/src/features/sections/billing-state.ts:23`). The backend status contract has no billing-origin field and no RevenueCat webhook/receipt reconciliation was found in app/lib source. This is not a working native subscription system.

**If store plans ship:** implement product/interval catalogue mapping, visible purchase/restore/manage controls, server-owned tenant entitlement reconciliation, validated provider events, replay/out-of-order handling, renewals/refunds/revocations/grace periods, account mapping and cross-platform restore. Prevent duplicate Stripe/store subscriptions using durable billing origin and authoritative subscription state; `has_customer` alone is not a complete entitlement model. Bind cache/SDK identity before displaying or acting on purchased access. `syncPurchasesIdentity` currently catches identity failure while retaining whichever SDK identity exists (`mobile/src/lib/purchases.ts:154`); fix and test before using it for access/purchase decisions. Verify sandbox purchase, cancellation, restore after reinstall, account switch, lost response and delayed webhook without production charges.

**If a free owner companion ships:** deliberately scope the app around existing service access and provide honest account/plan status. Remove disabled implementation-detail notices and review every portal/help/acquisition path for purchase calls to action that conflict with the chosen storefront/model. The current Stripe portal endpoint promises plan management and selects the default portal configuration (`web/app/api/billing/portal/route.ts:1`; `web/lib/stripe/billing.ts:192`). Actual portal upgrade controls were not exercised. A restricted payment-method/history/cancellation journey is materially different from a buy/upgrade funnel; configure and document the real behavior. Do not assume that describing the audience as businesses automatically qualifies individual tradie subscriptions for the enterprise exception.

The primary content is private business/customer work, not an anonymous social feed. Do not invent a public moderation product as a release requirement from the mere existence of messaging. Verify access control, support/report escalation, retention and any future publicly shared user-generated material according to its actual audience.

### Technical Stability & Performance

Versioned Expo documentation aligns SDK 54 with React Native 0.81 and React 19.1, which matches the package manifest. That compatibility fact is not proof the exact application builds successfully. SDK 54's minimum supported Xcode is not Apple's current upload minimum. [Expo SDK 54 reference](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v54.0.0/)

Since 28 April 2026, Apple's submission requirement is Xcode 26 or later and an iOS 26 SDK for iOS uploads. The local EAS production profile does not pin an iOS image; automatic image selection may already meet the requirement. Verify the actual candidate build log/SDK rather than claiming an obligatory Expo upgrade or a known build failure. Complete updated age-rating answers; if distributing in the EU, verify trader status. [Apple upcoming requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)

`eas.json` already contains production channel, automatic build-number increment and a submit profile. `app.json` contains bundle identity, assets, OTA URL/runtime policy and camera/biometric/notification plugins. Empty tracked submit config is not evidence that App Store Connect credentials or app identity are absent remotely. Verify signing, archive validation, store metadata, privacy answers, screenshots, export-compliance answers and upload status against the actual App Store Connect record.

Required physical-device scenarios: fresh install; restored/expired Clerk session; low/no network; API timeout/partial failures; camera denied/limited library/cancel; deep link while signed-out/signed-in/wrong-account/biometrically locked; external provider cancellation; background/terminated upload; cache isolation; quote approval once after a lost response; push tap after sign-out and re-sign-in; font scaling/VoiceOver/keyboard avoidance. Browser Playwright and mocked native modules are narrower evidence.

### UX & Reviewability

The native welcome screen explains the core purpose and provides signup/sign-in/support (`WelcomeScreen.tsx:23-55`). The support form is native, preserves an encrypted draft and reports server-confirmed delivery (`SupportScreen.tsx:76,99,283`). These improve review access and should be preserved.

Provide a dedicated account already provisioned with realistic but non-customer data, all reviewable trade tools enabled, and predictable quote/chat/payment history. Signup depends on invitation/account/provisioning state; reviewers should not require access to an employee inbox or a real customer phone. Provide working credentials and any permitted verification arrangement through App Store Connect, not in source control. The backend and providers needed for the demonstrated features must be available throughout review. [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
